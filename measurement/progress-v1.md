# Measuring personal progress — v1.0

These definitions are proposed for personal tracking; the custom items and combined workflow have not been psychometrically validated. Numbers are observations or judgments, not objective truth. Read [research notes](../research/psychology-and-measurement.md) for original studies and limitations.

## What to measure

| Construct | Collection and exact definition | Direction and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **Task-specific self-confidence** | Immediately before the first take, answer: “How confident are you that you can explain today's main point and two supporting ideas clearly to the stated audience in 1–2 minutes?” 0 = not at all confident; 10 = completely confident. | Higher = greater belief in ability for this task, not a measure of actual skill. It is a custom one-item measure informed by task-specific self-efficacy theory, not a validated scale. If the task has different requirements, record the exact question and do not silently compare. |
| **Nervousness** | Immediately before the first take: “How nervous do you feel about giving this particular talk right now?” 0 = not at all nervous; 10 = extremely nervous. | Lower = less self-reported nervousness, not necessarily better performance. A custom one-item self-report, not PRPSA or diagnosis. Optional; can be left blank. |
| **Listener understanding** | Before speaking, write intended main point privately. A listener who has not seen it or the assessment watches/listens once, then writes the main point in their own words. You and/or a named second reviewer compare the response to the prewritten point and mark `matched`, `partly matched`, `not matched`, or `unable to judge`. Save rationale and role. | Track counts of each label and eligible listener responses, e.g. 2 matched / 3 eligible. This is a custom outcome check, not a validated comprehension test. Listeners vary; AI must never be presented as human. Prefer the same listener over time and note their topic familiarity; a single listener is not representative. |
| **Task structure** | On a complete first take, use the context rubric to judge whether the pre-recorded main point, two relevant supporting points and brief close are present and identifiable. Record each `yes`, `no`, or `uncertain` with timestamps and reviewer; overall target met only when all four are `yes`. | Track met / assessed talks; uncertain or incomplete sessions are excluded and reported separately. Presence of a checklist element does not prove effectiveness. This target applies to the specific practice task, not every communication situation. |
| **Speaking performance** | Rate the eight custom rubric dimensions separately on 1–4 only if sufficient evidence exists. Record evidence, exact rubric version, reviewer, and verifier status. | An ordinal rating and judgment, not a precise numerical proficiency score. Report individual ratings and within-criterion distributions; avoid treating a +1 as a calibrated amount of improvement. |
| **Daily practice / transfer** | Log date, prompt, first take vs rehearsal, format, audience, preparation and whether applicable a real presentation occurred. | Frequency shows exposure and context, not quality or improvement. Do not compare a solo-camera session to a live high-stakes presentation as if equivalent. |

Supplementary acoustics such as speech rate or filler counts are exploratory only: state the counting procedure, speech duration and transcript reliability; no universal good target. Never use them as proxies for confidence or intelligence. Word choice, dialect and accent are not defects on their own.

## Timing and comparison rules

- **Baseline:** three distinct comparable first-take prompts, under consistent conditions, over the initial week if practical. Sample size 3 is useful orientation, not a statistically stable baseline. Save all data, even an awkward take.
- **Each week:** aim for up to three comparable fresh first takes. The aim is a personal convention, not a guaranteed reliable sample. If fewer exist, report the actual number. For confidence and nervousness report all values and, when available, the median and range. For structure report successes/assessed and uncertain count; for listener understanding report all category counts/eligible. For ordinal ratings report criterion-by-criterion values, frequency of each rating and reviewer status.
- Compare the same prompt family and version, language, length range, preparation, audience type, first take, similar topic familiarity and equipment. Log changes in rubric, reviewer, ASR and conditions. Fresh comparable prompts reduce repetition effects but differ in difficulty. When material conditions differ, show them side by side marked `not directly comparable`.
- Baseline-to-weekly trends are tentative; three observations do not produce a trustworthy precision interval or prove the practice caused change. Look for repeated patterns over multiple comparable sessions plus listener evidence. Preserve missing values rather than averaging them as zero.
- Keep before-speech confidence and nervousness distinct from optional post-speech reflections; do not compare pre to post as if they were the same measure. Version changes to questions/scales, including switching from the previous proposed 1–5 comfort item, start a new series: **no 1–5 to 0–10 conversion**.
- Report uncertainty and who rated each observation. AI-review confidence is not the speaker's self-confidence. Low nervousness, fluent speech, and listener understanding can move independently.

## Illustrative calculation — fictional data

Baseline three pre-talk confidence values: 3, 4, 5 → median 4, range 3–5. Week 4: 5, 6, 7 → median 6, range 5–7. Structure: 1/3 baseline vs 2/3 in week 4; listener main point matched 1/2 baseline vs 2/3 week 4. These figures are invented solely to show how to present results. They do not establish your baseline, statistical significance or a causal effect, and the small denominators must remain visible.

## Suggested initial target

Over four weeks, on two of three fresh comparable first takes, demonstrate the task structure checklist, with evidence checked by you or another reviewer. Track confidence, nervousness and listener understanding alongside it as separate outcomes; do not force them to move together. Adjust the target after real baseline results and your preferences.
