# Baseline, calibration and quality tests — v1

This is a proposed trial protocol. None of the evaluator, verifier, scores, or tests has been implemented or validated yet.

## Week 0: personal baseline

Record three separate unscripted 1–2 minute first takes on different but similarly difficult prompts. Do not restart until the first take has been saved. Specify the audience and goal for each. Example prompts:

1. Explain a project you're working on to a colleague unfamiliar with it.
2. Recommend a change to a familiar process to a small team, and explain why.
3. Explain a concept you recently learned to a curious friend.

Before each, complete the separate task-specific confidence and nervousness questions in [the measurement plan](progress-v1.md); an optional post-speech reflection is a different observation. These are personal check-ins, not objective measurements. Save original media, recording conditions, prompt and versions. Mark incomplete or noisy recordings. Listening comprehension, interaction and formal proficiency are outside the scope of these monologues.

Have the evaluator produce one report per recording. Review the cited audio passages yourself and correct ASR. Identify recurring strengths and select just one high-impact practice objective. Do not treat an AI score, any single take, or the lowest dimension as a definitive diagnosis.

## Proposed four-week practice target

On two of three fresh, comparable presentation prompts, make the main point identifiable, provide two relevant supporting points and finish with a brief close. You or a listener should judge the target from recordings with the same criteria, separately from the AI's assessment. This is an initial practice target chosen for your goals, not an established benchmark; revise it after your real baseline. A small set of prompts is a useful practice check, not proof of broad improvement.

Daily or near-daily: 2 minutes of a first take, review one timestamp, 3–5 minutes of focused rehearsal, then an optional second take. Compare first takes to first takes; label rehearsals. Weekly: summarize recurring strengths, the chosen practice target and evidence, plus how comfortable you felt. Avoid equating a repeated familiar prompt with general improvement.

## External anchors

Optionally take EF SET's 90-minute four-skill test for an external English proficiency anchor, save the date and actual per-skill results separately, and follow EF's current retake policy. The test is not a complete measure of presentation skill. Alternatively ask a teacher or speech evaluator to rate selected samples and discuss differences; describe the person's expertise and the rubric they actually used. Do not imply an official certification or map custom 1–4 ratings to CEFR levels.

## Test the verifiers before trusting them

Create a small consented review set from several real recordings and deliberately constructed examples. Include strong and weak examples, different topics, normal accents, natural pauses, very short audio, noise, an incomplete clip, a silent clip, overlapping speakers, transcript mistakes, missing audience context and video without usable audio. Mark illustrative examples as synthetic; never report them as your personal results. Human-reviewed evidence and ratings are the reference for the pilot, not assumed universal ground truth.

For each test case, define expected eligible criteria, reference transcript for checked passages, evidence timestamps, a human rating where feasible, and a reason for the judgment. Keep at least some examples held out when adjusting prompts. Test:

- **Evidence integrity:** invalid/out-of-range timestamps, altered quotes and missing hashes should be rejected; exact transcript match must not be mistaken for audio verification.
- **Missing inputs:** lack of audio prevents intelligibility and fluency ratings; lack of audience context prevents audience-fit rating; a monologue cannot rate interaction. Truncated clips cannot prove that an opening or close is absent.
- **ASR errors:** inject a wrong word and a lost filler; compare the reviewer to original audio, and check that uncertain text is not converted into a confident criticism or false count.
- **Reproducibility and bias:** repeat scoring, anonymize/order-swap recordings for comparative judgments, compare task-equivalent samples with different recording quality, and inspect differences across accents without expecting identical delivery. Log the changes and individual disagreements.
- **Human comparison:** collect a blind review by a willing listener or qualified coach for a subset. Record exact agreement, adjacent-category agreement, and disagreements by criterion; a small sample only gives preliminary signals. Ask listeners what the main point was without showing the evaluator's answer.
- **Usefulness:** after trying a drill, ask whether the feedback was understandable, accurate and actionable. Keep user disagreement as evidence, not as an error to override.

Before regular use, review the observed error cases with the user and agree on fit-for-purpose thresholds *after* seeing a representative pilot; report the sample sizes and failure counts. Do not claim calibration or a performance threshold was achieved without results. Track both false criticism and missed opportunities. Revise and version the rubric only with the user's approval; re-run the held-out cases before comparing versions.
