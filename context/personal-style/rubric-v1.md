# Personal style rubric — draft v1

This is a proposed, unvalidated coaching rubric derived from your goals; it is not an exam or a creator similarity score. Rate **only** the communication task and available evidence. Use: **1** significant difficulty; **2** developing; **3** mostly effective; **4** consistently effective for this task. `N/A` means the task does not require the criterion; `not assessed` means required evidence is missing or insufficient. No overall score or fixed creator imitation.

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Needed evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Approachable connection** | The explanation consistently lacks context for the listener or uses a dismissive tone that makes following it difficult. | Some listener-oriented cues exist, but unexplained assumptions or framing create distance. | Language and context generally welcome the intended listener; occasional mismatches. | Context, wording and examples consistently include the intended listener without forced familiarity. | Audience and task plus enough speech. Evaluator's impression is a judgment, not proof of how the audience feels. Collect listener impressions separately. |
| **Simplify while preserving meaning** | The central idea is hard to identify or the explanation materially distorts it. | One part is explained, but major terms, steps or necessary limitations remain unclear. | Main idea and most essential steps are clear, with only a minor gap. | Main idea, necessary terms and steps are clear without removing material qualifications. | Clear topic, goal and evidence for factual accuracy. If accuracy cannot be checked, mark that component uncertain; listener comprehension and accuracy are separate. |
| **Concrete explanation** | Abstract statements lack an example or application when needed to follow them. | An example is present but its connection is unclear or misleading. | A relevant example generally clarifies the point; the link may need a little work. | A well-chosen example makes the idea clear and its link and important limits are explained where needed. | Sufficient speech and a task where an example is relevant. |
| **Narrative movement** | A story is difficult to follow and its relationship to the point is unclear. | A situation or event is identifiable but the change, consequence or point is missing/unclear. | A listener can generally follow what happened and why it matters. | Context, development and outcome/insight work together and serve the purpose. | Complete story-oriented task and enough of the story; otherwise N/A or not assessed. No requirement to manufacture drama. |
| **Expressive delivery** | A relevant message is consistently difficult to follow because emphasis or audible delivery obscures it. | Some emphasis or visual movement supports the message, but its connection is often unclear or distracting in context. | Audible emphasis and, where available, visible gestures generally support key ideas without obscuring them. | Delivery consistently highlights important ideas and supports comprehension while remaining appropriate to the task. | Original audio; video only for visual observations. Report audio and visual observations separately. Low energy, disability, accent or limited visible motion are not automatic defects. |
| **Natural fit for the speaker** | Speaker reports the approach felt very forced and would not want to use it again. | Speaker reports noticeable discomfort or unnatural elements. | Speaker reports it mostly felt like their own style, with some awkwardness. | Speaker reports it felt natural and repeatable for this situation. | Speaker's own description; evaluator cannot infer this from appearance. Optional; otherwise not assessed. |

For each rating cite the observation, timestamp, modality, reason it supports the selected anchor, reviewer identity, limitations and one possible improvement. A 1 or 4 needs an additional example when feasible; otherwise note limited coverage. Do not conclude warmth, confidence, enthusiasm, personality, emotional state or audience impact from facial expressions. A real listener's reaction and the speaker's own experience are separate evidence, not facts inferred by the model. Fast speaking is acceptable only where understandable; never set a fixed speed target.

## Optional listener questions (custom, not validated)

After one listen, without showing them the intended point, scores or desired style:

1. What was the main idea in your own words?
2. What example or part helped you understand it? What remained unclear?
3. How did the speaker's way of explaining feel to you? Optional prompt: Did it feel approachable or formal, and why?
4. If a story was used, what happened and why did it matter?

Record the exact listener response, who they are and topic familiarity, then compare their first answer to the speaker's previously written intended point. Do not label an AI listener as human. One listener does not represent every audience. Multiple blinded responses are useful when available; list counts and differing views, not a fabricated consensus.
