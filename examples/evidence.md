# Fictional evidence examples

These are made-up illustrations, not recordings or results for the user. Do not copy the example's topic, phrasing, score or conclusion into a real assessment.

- **Useful strength:** “00:08–00:16: The speaker states the purpose before explaining the details.” Source: audio, checked by [name/status]. Why it matters: the listener knows what to expect.
- **Useful opportunity:** “00:33–00:47: Two ideas follow one another without an explicit connection. A transition such as ‘This leads to the next point’ may help.” Source: reviewed audio; judgment: interpretation.
- **Not acceptable:** “You looked nervous, so you lack confidence.” A facial observation does not establish mental state.
- **Not acceptable:** “You said X incorrectly” based solely on uncertain ASR. Recheck audio before attributing a wording issue to the speaker.
- **Not assessed:** listener understanding when no listener response exists; fluency when only a text transcript exists.
