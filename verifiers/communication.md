## 1. Combined Dual-Author Persona & Video Auditing Philosophy
- **Judge Names & Role:** Barbara Minto & Chip & Dan Heath — Strict Multimodal Video Diagnostic Auditors
- **Book 1 Core Lens (Barbara Minto):** Ruthlessly hunts for structural chaos, bottom-up rambling, ungrouped lists exceeding five items, overlapping (non-MECE) categories, and intellectually blank assertions that force the audience to do the cognitive heavy lifting.
- **Book 2 Core Lens (Chip & Dan Heath):** Ruthlessly hunts for the "Curse of Knowledge"—abstract corporate buzzwords, un-anchored raw statistics, predictable openings, and feature-heavy explanations that fail to trigger visual, emotional, or narrative memory hooks.
- **Unified Synthesis Philosophy:** Minto’s Pyramid Principle audits the *architectural skeleton* of the presentation (ensuring top-down logical rigor and visual slide-to-speech alignment), while the Heath Brothers’ SUCCESs framework audits the *cognitive flesh* of the delivery (ensuring every logical node is concrete, unexpected, credible, emotional, and story-driven).

## 2. The 20-Point Multimodal Video Diagnostic Checklist

| Rule ID | Author & Chapter | Modality (Transcript / Camera / Audio) | Exact Video Failure Trigger (With Timestamp/Frame Criteria) | Severity (-2 / -5 / -10 pts) | Exact Fix Rule (How to correct it on camera/mic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **01** | **Minto, Ch. 1 & 3:** Ordering from the Top Down (*30-Second Rule*) | **Transcript / Audio** | Speaker fails to state the governing conclusion (Main Point / Answer) and the preview of Key Line points within the **first 30 to 45 seconds** of speaking, instead narrating chronological findings or data discovery first. | **Critical (-10 pts)** | State the governing conclusion and Key Line summary within the first 30 seconds before elaborating on any supporting evidence. |
| **02** | **Minto, Ch. 2 & 4:** The Introductory Flow (*S-C-Q Story Form*) | **Transcript** | Speaker jumps straight into the thesis or data within the **first 20 seconds** without first establishing a non-controversial **Situation** (time/place anchor) and a **Complication** (problem/trigger) that raises the audience's Question. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Open with a 15–30 second S-C-Q narrative: state the known Situation, introduce the Complication that went wrong, and deliver the Main Point as the Answer. |
| **03** | **Minto, Ch. 1 & 6:** The Magical Number Seven (*5-Point Grouping Cap*) | **Transcript / Camera** | Speaker presents a horizontal grouping of more than **5 enumerated points** in a single spoken list or on a single visual slide without sub-grouping them into higher-level categories. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Cap all spoken and visual groupings at 3 to 5 items maximum; if more exist, cluster them under 2–3 intermediate summary headings. |
| **04** | **Minto, Ch. 7:** Summarizing Grouped Ideas (*Intellectually Blank Assertions*) | **Transcript / Audio** | Speaker introduces a section using a vacuous category label (e.g., *"There are three problems," "I have four findings,"* or *"Let's look at background"*) instead of stating a specific, falsifiable summary insight. | **Critical (-10 pts)** | Replace plural-noun category announcements with a direct summary sentence that states the *implication* of the similarity or the *effect* of the actions below it. |
| **05** | **Minto, Ch. 6:** Structural Order (*MECE Violation*) | **Transcript / Camera** | Spoken sub-points or on-screen diagram boxes overlap conceptually (not Mutually Exclusive) or omit a major functional component of the whole system being divided (not Collectively Exhaustive). | **Critical (-10 pts)** | Audit sibling points before speaking to ensure zero conceptual overlap and 100% coverage of the parent topic's scope. |
| **06** | **Minto, Ch. 5:** Deduction vs. Induction (*Key Line Mystery Story*) | **Transcript / Audio** | Speaker uses a slow, chained deductive argument at the top Key Line level (*"Here is what is wrong -> Here is what causes it -> Therefore here is what to do"*), delaying the action recommendation past the **90-second mark**. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Present Key Line points inductively (lead with the 3–4 direct action recommendations first, then support each with its respective cause/evidence). |
| **07** | **Minto, Ch. 7:** State the Effect of Actions (*Vague Action Verbs*) | **Transcript** | Speaker states action steps or recommendations using open-ended verbs (*"strengthen," "improve," "reduce," "review"*) without specifying a tangible, visualizable end-product or numerical/date cutoff. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Word every action point to state the exact end-product you can "hold in your hand" when finished (e.g., *"Cut loan processing time from 2 days to 2 hours by Q3"*). |
| **08** | **Minto, Ch. 10 & 11:** Parallel Grammatical Form | **Camera / Transcript** | On-screen bullet points or spoken enumerated lists mix grammatical structures within the same grouping (e.g., Point 1 starts with an imperative verb, Point 2 with a gerund *"-ing"*, Point 3 with a noun phrase). | **Minor (-2 pts)** | Enforce strict syntactic parallelism across all sibling points on slides and in speech (e.g., start every bullet in the set with an active imperative verb). |
| **09** | **Minto, Ch. 11:** Designing Text Slides (*Visual Recitation & Slide Bloat*) | **Camera / Audio** | On-screen text slide exceeds **6 lines or 30 words**, OR the speaker's eye-line locks onto the screen/monitor for **>4 consecutive seconds** while reading the slide text verbatim to the camera. | **Critical (-10 pts)** | Restrict text slides to <30 words/6 lines as stark visual aids, maintain eye contact with the camera lens, and deliver the fuller narrative via spoken script. |
| **10** | **Minto, Ch. 11:** Designing Exhibit Slides (*Caption Titles on Charts*) | **Camera** | Speaker displays a data chart, graph, or diagram on screen capped with a non-verbal topic caption (e.g., *"Sales Outlook"* or *"Share of Profits by Region"*) rather than a full declarative sentence or verb phrase. | **Minor (-2 pts)** | Title every visual chart or exhibit with a complete statement containing a verb that states the exact takeaway (e.g., *"Western Region accounts for half of profits"*). |
| **11** | **Heath, Intro & Ch. 3:** The Curse of Knowledge (*Abstract Buzzword Density*) | **Transcript / Audio** | Speaker utters **3 or more abstract corporate/academic buzzwords** (e.g., *"synergy," "paradigm," "unlocking value," "infrastructure optimization"*) within any **60-second window** without a sensory, human-action example. | **Critical (-10 pts)** | Strip out Expertese and translate every abstract concept into concrete human actions and sensory nouns that a novice can visualize immediately. |
| **12** | **Heath, Ch. 1:** Finding the Core (*Decision Paralysis / Multi-Goal Dilution*) | **Transcript** | Speaker presents multiple competing "top priorities" or caveats without isolating a single overarching **Commander's Intent** (*"The single most important thing we must do is..."*). | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Force ruthless prioritization by explicitly stating one singular, overarching goal (Commander's Intent) that trumps all secondary trade-offs. |
| **13** | **Heath, Ch. 1:** Simple = Core + Compact (*Un-Anchored Technical Explanation*) | **Transcript** | Speaker spends **>30 seconds** listing raw technical attributes or mechanics of an unfamiliar concept without anchoring it to a preexisting audience **schema** or **generative analogy** (*"X is basically a [familiar concept] with [twist]"*). | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Anchor any new or complex concept immediately in an existing schema using a compact analogy or high-concept pitch before explaining mechanics. |
| **14** | **Heath, Ch. 2:** Unexpectedness (*Monotone Pattern / Zero Schema Violation*) | **Audio / Camera** | Speaker delivers the **first 60 seconds** at a flat, predictable vocal pace and pitch with zero counterintuitive twist, pattern break, or violation of the audience's "guessing machine." | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Break the audience's guessing machine early by exposing an "uncommon sense" implication, accompanied by a vocal pause and pitch shift. |
| **15** | **Heath, Ch. 2:** Avoiding Gimmickry (*Non-Postdictable Shock*) | **Transcript / Camera** | Speaker uses a jarring joke, prop, or dramatic visual hook that consumes **>15 seconds** of video time but has zero logical connection to the core message (gimmicky surprise without insight). | **Minor (-2 pts)** | Ensure every surprise or pattern-break is strictly "post-dictable"—it must directly target and illuminate the core message once resolved. |
| **16** | **Heath, Ch. 2:** The Gap Theory of Curiosity (*Premature Answer Dumping*) | **Transcript / Audio** | Speaker dumps a block of factual answers or solutions for **>60 seconds** without first posing a mystery, puzzle, or explicit knowledge gap (*"Why did X happen?"*) to make the audience feel the need for closure. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Open a knowledge gap before closing it: pose a specific puzzle, paradox, or prediction challenge before revealing the factual answer. |
| **17** | **Heath, Ch. 3 & 4:** Concrete Props & Testable Credentials (*Zero "See-for-Yourself" Proof*) | **Camera / Transcript** | Speaker makes a bold or counterintuitive claim during the video without either displaying a tangible physical prop/demo on camera or offering a **Testable Credential** (*"Try/check this for yourself"*). | **Minor (-2 pts)** | Bring a concrete physical prop into the camera frame or challenge the viewer with a falsifiable "see-for-yourself" test or **Sinatra Test** example. |
| **18** | **Heath, Ch. 4:** The Human-Scale Principle (*Un-Contextualized Raw Statistics*) | **Transcript** | Speaker cites **3 or more raw numbers, percentages, or large-scale figures** (e.g., millions/billions) within **60 seconds** without translating them into an everyday human-scale comparison or relationship. | **Critical (-10 pts)** | Never leave raw numbers naked; translate statistics into a human-scale relationship or everyday analogy (e.g., minutes per day, soft drinks per month). |
| **19** | **Heath, Ch. 5:** The Mother Teresa Principle & WIIFY (*Analytical Hat / Feature Trap*) | **Transcript** | Speaker discusses a problem using only macro-level group data or product features without naming a **single specific individual** or using second-person **"You" visualization** (*"Imagine yourself..."*) to spell out the WIIFY. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Take off the Analytical Hat: spotlight one named individual's plight and use "Imagine you..." scenarios to connect features to personal/higher-Maslow benefits. |
| **20** | **Heath, Ch. 6:** Stories as Flight Simulators (*Moral-Only Advice*) | **Transcript** | Speaker delivers abstract rules or "lessons learned" (e.g., *"Keep communication open"*) without recounting a step-by-step **process narrative** (Challenge, Connection, or Creativity plot) showing how a protagonist overcame obstacles. | **Moderate (-5 pts)** | Replace abstract advice slogans with a "flight simulator" story that walks the listener chronologically through the specific events, obstacles, and actions taken. |

## 3. Unified CEFR / EF SET Video Tier Benchmarks (0–100 Scale)

- **A1–A2 (1–40 pts): Novice / High error density**
  - **Profile:** The speaker is trapped in their own head, delivering a disorganized, bottom-up data dump plagued by the Curse of Knowledge.
  - **Observable Failures:** Fails to state the main conclusion in the first 45 seconds (**Rule 01**) and lacks any S-C-Q introductory framing (**Rule 02**). Groupings are rambling lists of 6+ items (**Rule 03**) capped with intellectually blank assertions like *"Here are my findings"* (**Rule 04**) that overlapillogically (**Rule 05**). Visually and acoustically, the speaker reads verbatim from 60-word text slides with eye-contact broken for >4 seconds (**Rule 09**), spouts dense abstract buzzwords (**Rule 11**), and buries the audience in un-contextualized raw statistics (**Rule 18**) with a flat, predictable delivery (**Rule 14**).

- **B1–B2 (41–60 pts): Functional / Inconsistent execution**
  - **Profile:** The speaker has a recognizable structure and avoids reading slides verbatim, but relies on slow deductive buildup and abstract "Expertese" rather than sticky, concrete communication.
  - **Observable Failures:** Establishes a basic top-down point (**Rule 01**) and keeps text slides brief (**Rule 09**), but delays recommendations by using a slow deductive mystery-story structure on the Key Line (**Rule 06**) and words action steps with vague verbs like *"improve"* or *"strengthen"* (**Rule 07**). On the stickiness front, the speaker fails to isolate a single Commander's Intent (**Rule 12**), dumps answers without opening curiosity gaps (**Rule 16**), explains technical concepts without schema analogies (**Rule 13**), and relies on macro-level abstractions or moralizing tips instead of individual WIIFYs (**Rule 19**) and flight-simulator stories (**Rule 20**).

- **C1 (61–70 pts): Advanced / Minor mechanical slips**
  - **Profile:** The speaker commands both top-down pyramidal logic and concrete, story-driven stickiness, suffering only from minor visual formatting or secondary polish slips.
  - **Observable Failures:** Zero Critical (-10 pt) violations. The speaker leads with an S-C-Q intro and inductive Key Line (**Rules 01, 02, 06**), uses concrete end-product wording (**Rule 07**), translates statistics to human scale (**Rule 18**), and embeds flight-simulator stories (**Rule 20**). Deductions are limited to minor mechanical slips: a slide or spoken list breaks strict grammatical parallelism (**Rule 08**), a chart uses a noun-caption title instead of a verb statement (**Rule 10**), an opening hook borders on a non-postdictable gimmick (**Rule 15**), or the speaker misses an opportunity to show a physical prop / testable credential on camera (**Rule 17**).

- **C2 (71–100 pts): Mastery / Zero structural, vocal, or visual violations**
  - **Profile:** Flawless synthesis of Minto’s logical architecture and the Heath Brothers’ cognitive stickiness.
  - **Observable Execution:** Within the first 30–45 seconds, the speaker establishes a crisp S-C-Q story (**Rule 02**), opens a compelling curiosity gap (**Rule 16**), and delivers a single, proverb-like Commander's Intent (**Rules 01, 12**). Every Key Line grouping is inductive (**Rule 06**), capped at 3–5 MECE points (**Rules 03, 05**), phrased in parallel end-product language (**Rules 07, 08**), and introduced with a sharp summary insight (**Rule 04**). Visually and vocally, the speaker maintains camera engagement over sparse, verb-titled visual exhibits (**Rules 09, 10**), anchors complex ideas in familiar schemas (**Rule 13**), humanizes every statistic (**Rule 18**), appeals to elevated identity/WIIFY (**Rule 19**), and drives action through vivid flight-simulator stories and testable credentials (**Rules 17, 20**).
